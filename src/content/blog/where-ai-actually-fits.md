Most of the AI advice aimed at small businesses falls into one of two camps: "AI will transform everything" or "AI is overhyped nonsense." Neither is useful when you're trying to run an actual business.

Here's a simpler way to think about it.

## Start with the bottleneck, not the tool

Don't ask "how can I use AI?" Ask "what's actually slow, expensive, or annoying right now?" Then check whether AI is a good fit for that specific problem. Sometimes it is — drafting first passes of routine writing, summarizing long documents, handling repetitive research. Sometimes it isn't, and the honest answer is a better process, not a new tool.

## Three questions worth asking

1. **Is the task repetitive and well-defined?** AI is good at patterns it's seen before. It's a poor fit for judgment calls that depend on context only you have.
2. **Can you check the output quickly?** If verifying the AI's work takes as long as doing it yourself, you haven't saved anything.
3. **What's the cost of a mistake?** Low-stakes drafts are a great place to experiment. Client-facing commitments or financial decisions deserve more caution — and a human in the loop.

## The honest version

Some of what I do in an initial conversation with a business owner is talk them out of an AI project that isn't worth the effort, and toward one that is. That's not a sales pitch — it's the actual job. If a spreadsheet template or a cleaner intake process would solve your problem faster than an AI agent, I'll say so.
