Ask most advisors how a project went and you'll get one number: revenue, margin, ROI. It's not wrong to track that — it's just incomplete.

Cedar Wave Advisory works from a triple bottom line: financial, social, and environmental. Three lines, not one.

## What that looks like in practice

It doesn't mean every project needs a sustainability report attached. It means the questions get asked earlier. Does this automation save time, or does it just shift the tedious part onto someone else? Does this growth plan work for the team doing the work, not just the owner's margin? Is the "efficient" option actually cheaper once you count what it costs the community around it?

Sometimes the answer is: the financially optimal choice is also the right one, full stop. Good — that's an easy call. But when it isn't, it's worth knowing that before you commit, not after.

## Why this matters more with AI in the mix

AI tools make it easy to optimize hard for one number, fast — cut costs, cut time, cut headcount. That's real leverage, and I use it. But leverage without a wider lens tends to produce short-term wins with long-term costs: a support system nobody in the community can maintain, a process that hollows out the judgment your team used to bring.

Grounded doesn't mean slow. It means checking all three lines before you call something a win.
